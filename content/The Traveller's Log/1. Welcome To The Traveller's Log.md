Welcome to **The Traveler’s Log**. A **tabletop roleplaying game** (or TTRPG) that is built for story telling with a set of strong core rules. The Traveler’s Log is designed to be easy to understand and enable players to create their ideal character suited to many different power fantasies. There are a plethora of abilities to choose from that you can mix and match to your heart’s content to create the perfect power fantasy that you like!

When you embark on your travels, you are sure to come across many trials, experience hardships, victories, loss, and much more. How? With the guidance of your **game master**!

As you read this book, you will learn the flow of play, how to create a character, and how to embark your first journey in The Traveller’s Log!
# Common Terminology

These are some common terms with a short description to help you get up to speed if you aren’t familiar with these already.

**Player Character.** A Player Character (or **PC** for short) is a character designed by a player, carefully put together with a large list of options who they will take control of during adventures. The player in control of this PC names this character, chooses their bloodline, gender, and many more details about it! Explore different ideas, try different bloodlines, roleplay the dream character you want to! If you want to go ahead and get started creating your character, [[3. Character Creation|go here]].

**[[11. Game Master's Tools|Game Master]].** A Game Master (or **GM** for short) has a big role in The Traveller’s Log. Through the guidance of your GM, the party will travel the lands, face dangerous beasts and bandits, experience hardships and triumphs… All facilitated by your GM. **The GM is not your direct adversary**, but rather, a story teller and referee that makes sure the adventure doesn’t bend the laws of reality (unless you can actually do that)! The GM will control all Non-Player Characters.

**Non-Player Character.** A Non-Player Character (or **NPC** for short) is always going to be under the control of the GM. NPCs serve the purpose of helping the world around the party feel alive. These characters range from villagers, to kings, to bandits, to even dangerous beasts! How the party interacts with these NPCs is completely up to them. Though, sometimes it may not be if they get charmed, frightened, or otherwise enthralled by these NPCs.

**The Party.** This is the most common term you will see when something refers to the group of players that are adventuring together. Typically parties range from 3 to 5 players.

**[[2. Core Rules#Skill checks|Skill Checks]].** This is a tool that The Traveller’s Log utilizes whenever a situation does not have a clear outcome. It would be too easy if everything was handed to you on a silver platter, so we use skill checks to see if you succeed at your plan or not! You will make skill checks when called by the rules or when the GM asks you to make one. Roll your dice and see what happens next!

**Character Sheet.** Every player will have one of these – whether it’s on a loose piece of paper, a pre-designed character sheet, or some kind of stone tablet that they’ve found and etched into, you will want one of these to record all of the details about your character so you can easily reference them when needed!

**Tokens.** These can be anything, but they will be useful when tracking duration of effects, limited use abilities, and similar. Whatever you have an abundance of can serve the purpose of these and will help you not lose track of that useful effect you’re keeping an eye on.
# Dice Notation

**This is important to learn, make sure you understand dice notation!**

There are seven types of dice you will be using in The Traveller’s Log:
- Four-sided dice (or **d4**)
- Six-sided dice (or **d6**)
- Eight-sided dice (or **d8**)
- Ten-sided dice (or **d10**)
- Twelve-sided dice (or **d12**)
- Twenty-sided dice (or **d20**)
- Percent dice (or **d100** **or** **d%**); Primarily for GM

Dice notation indicates how many and what dice size to roll.
- **"2d12"** means roll two twelve-sided dice and add the results.
- **"1d10+4"** means roll one ten-sided die and add 4 to the result.

**Keep Highest & Lowest:**
- Keep Highest **(kh):** When rolling multiple dice, you keep the single highest result. For example: Rolling “**3d12kh**” means to roll three twelve-sided dice and keep the single highest result you got of the three.
- Keep Lowest **(kl):** When rolling multiple dice, you keep the single highest result. For example: Rolling “**4d8kl**” means to roll four eight-sided dice and keep the single lowest result you got of the four.

**[[3. Character Creation#Prowess|Prowess Die]].** A lot of rules will call on your **[[3. Character Creation#Prowess|prowess die]]**. When it does so, the size and number of dice you roll is based on what your [[3. Character Creation#Prowess|prowess]] is and the notation that precedes it. Here is what the [[3. Character Creation#Prowess|prowess dice]] notation looks like:
- **“2pd”** means to roll 2 prowess dice. At levels 6, 7, and 8, this means to roll 2d8.
- **“3pd”** means to roll 3 prowess dice. At levels 9, 10, and 11, this means to roll 3d10.
