This chapter contains all of the core rules that The Traveller’s Log is built upon. Please take care to learn this information as it’s critical to have a good experience when playing The Traveller’s Log.

>**Important**: These rules are simply the _recommended_ way to play, but may be adjusted, removed, or added to by group consensus. When making changes, discuss them with all players and ensure they agree.
# Always Round Up
When dividing or multiplying numbers within TTL, **unless explicitly stated otherwise**, you will always round up to the nearest whole number. Anytime you see an ability or rule that mentions “half” or similar, it is included by this rule.
# Don’t use PEMDAS
When calculating the math in TTL, addition and subtraction comes first, then multipliers, then division.

**Add** → **Subtract** → **Multiply** → **Divide**

This order is primarily used in **[[#Damage Types|damage rolls]]** and **[[#Skill checks|skill checks]].**
# Overrides
Whenever a specific effect contradicts a general rule, that specific effect takes precedence over the general rule.

>**Example**: You normally cannot hold a two-handed weapon with one hand and use it effectively. However, the ability [[Titanic Grip]] allows you to do this.
# Order of Operations
When handling multiple effects that trigger in the same moment, they follow this order:

**[[1. Welcome To The Traveller's Log|Player Character]] → [[1. Welcome To The Traveller's Log|Non-Player Character]] → Environmental**

>**Note:** When multiple PC effects come from one player, the player may choose the order of which their effects happen. Should multiple PC effects come from more than one player, the players decide among themselves which order the effects happen. If a decision cannot be concluded, the GM will decide the order which the effects happen.
# Health And Energy
##### Health
**Health.** A character’s health is represented by a numeric value for the sake of measuring how well off they are in any given situation. A character at full health is completely fine from head to toe. A character at half health is moderately wounded; covered in wounds and bruises. A character at 1 health is barely standing, seemingly ready to collapse at the slightest wound, completely bloodied, battered, and/or mentally absent.

**Current Health** measures the damage a character can withstand before reaching their limit. At 0 health, [[#Surviving & Final Stand|a critical choice must be made]].

**Max Health** is the maximum vitality a character can have at any given time.
##### Wounded Threshold
The **wounded** threshold is equal to half of your max health (remember to always round up unless explicitly instructed otherwise). This value changes whenever your max health changes. Certain abilities and effects interact with being wounded.

>**Example:** The skill [[Execution]] grants you bonus damage against a wounded target.
##### Healthy
**Healthy** applies when a character is at full health and has no harmful status effects.
##### Energy
**Energy.** A character’s ability to perform powerful feats of magic and physical prowess is measured with energy. At full energy, a character is alert and ready. At half energy, a character is visibly worn out but can still act. At 0 energy, a character is winded and on the verge of collapse from overexertion.

**Current Energy** measures how much a character can exert before becoming exhausted.

**Max Energy** is the maximum endurance a character can maintain.

Energy is your primary resource for most actions. A character must have the exact amount of required energy to use an ability. For example, you cannot use an ability that costs 3 energy if you only have 2 energy remaining.
## Resting
**Long Rest:** A long rest requires at least 7 hours of rest and a ration’s worth of food. Failure to rest properly – even if your character might not need sleep – will result in [[#Exhaustion]]. For every 24 hours you do not full rest, you gain 1 stage of [[#Exhaustion]].

After a long rest, gain the following benefits:
- Recover all missing health and energy
- Recover from all status effects that naturally go away over time *(intoxicated, minor poison, etc).*
- Reduce [[#Exhaustion]] by 1 stage.

Should you be interrupted during your long rest, you will not gain the benefits of a full rest. However, you still **restore 1pd as health and 1d4 energy** upon this interruption.

**Short Rest:** A short rest requires at least 30 minutes of focused self-care. **Restore 1pd plus your [[3. Character Creation#Prowess|prowess level]] as health and an amount of energy equal to your [[3. Character Creation#Prowess|prowess level]].**

This may look like bandaging wounds, applying antidotes, and so on. **You may do this** **up to 3** **times** **before you must take a long rest.** If you are caught midway during a short rest, the attackers may get a [[#Surprise Round|surprise round]] against your party.
## Surviving & Final Stand
If a player character reaches 0 health, they must make a critical choice:
- **Final Stand:** The character accepts their impending death, pushing forward with the last of their willpower. As soon as they choose this option, they immediately perform **one uninterrupted action that always succeeds at the maximum potential**. This action cannot be blocked, dodged, or resisted in any capacity and it ignores any resource cost associated with the chosen action. Healing is impossible, as their life force has already faded. After the action concludes, the character dies. abilities that normally require focus will persist for their full duration even though the user has perished.
- **Survive:** If a player chooses to **survive**, their character falls unconscious for 1d12+3 hours. Upon waking up, the character restores **1d6+END** health (minimum 1). This choice comes with a long-lasting consequence.

>**Note**: Where the surviving character wakes could be a prison as they were captured, in the camp with their party as their body was hauled off with them, and so on.

### Consequences
The GM may roll on the consequence table or choose one as appropriate:

| d12   | Effect                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1-2   | -1 on _all_ rolls.                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| 3     | GM chooses a rare or precious item from your inventory – That item is stolen.                                                                                                                          |
| 4-7   | Roll a number of d6’s equal to the survivor’s [[3. Character Creation#Prowess\|prowess level]]: Your max health is reduced by the rolled amount. Health reduced this way cannot go below 1 max health. |
| 8-10  | Roll a number of d4’s equal to the survivor’s [[3. Character Creation#Prowess\|prowess level]]: Your max energy is reduced by the rolled amount. Energy reduced this way cannot go below 0 max energy. |
| 11-12 | **Roll a d10.**<br>**1-2:** AGI is reduced by 1.<br>**3-4:** MGT is reduced by 1.<br>**5-6:** END is reduced by 1.<br>**7-8:** WLL is reduced by 1.<br>**9-10:** WIS is reduced by 1.                  |
When a character is affected by one of these consequences, they must spend 1d20+10 days (rolled upon choosing to survive) resting, searching, or otherwise dedicating their time to recover from the consequence. Should a character suffer multiples of consequences, they are recovered from in the order they were received. You do not roll when an additional consequence is suffered while already affected by any consequences, instead, you roll a new duration (1d20+10) upon recovering from the previous consequence. The only exception being a stolen item: Stolen items may be foregone to begin recovering the next consequence, but the item cannot be found by spending your time as you would a normal consequence any longer. The GM may present a rare opportunity for you to recover this once stolen item, but that is at the GM’s discretion upon making this choice.

While under the effects of any of these consequences, it is _strongly_ _advised_ to have the character sit out for the duration of their trouble, as these consequences can stack. If you have 3 consequences (excluding stolen item consequences) stacked on your character and fall to 0 hp once more, **you** **no longer** **get a choice**, the character _must_ final stand and consequently die as a result.
# Skill checks
A **“skill check”** is a tool the GM uses to determine the outcomes of various situations. When asked to make a skill check, **unless specified otherwise**, you will roll 1d12 and add the relevant stat: **Agility, Might, Endurance, Will, or Wisdom**.

Skill checks are made **when required by the game rules**, such as by ability effects or **when asked by the GM.** The GM may impose a skill check based on circumstances around the character(s). Primarily when performing tasks with an uncertain outcome.

>**Example** **A**: An **AGI** skill check for a character with **+2 AGI** would be **1d12+2**. This results in a range of 3 to 14, with an average roll of 8½.

>**Example** **B**: A **WLL** skill check for a character with **+3 WLL** would roll **1d12+3**. This results in a range of 4 to 15, with an average roll of 9½.

Common uses for each stat during skill checks:
- **Agility (AGI).** Situations that require finesse or quick reflexes.
- **Might (MGT).** Situations that require raw strength, making large leaps, lifting heavy objects, or wrestling.
- **Endurance (END).** Withstanding intense weather or conditions.
- **Will (WLL).** Social situations, negotiation, intimidation, and self-control.
- **Wisdom (WIS).** Decision-making, lore, and awareness.
## Difficulty Ratings
The Difficulty Rating (**DR**) of the roll can be broken down as such:
- **Safe: DR 5 or less**. Most people can accomplish this task safely.
- **Normal: DR 7**. Moderately challenging.
- **Moderate: DR 10**. Requires a specialist to reliably complete.
- **Challenge: DR 13**. Exceptionally difficult; most cannot do this.
- **Master: DR 16**. Only the most skilled of people can succeed.
- **Impossible: DR 18 or more**. Only super humans can even think about trying this.

If you’re unsure what DR to set a skill check at as the GM, you can safely default to **DR 7**.
## Dice Sizes
Some effects and passives will note to increase or decrease the dice size roll. The dice sizes are:

**d4 ↔ d6 ↔ d8 ↔ d10 ↔ d12**

When something notes to _decrease_ the dice size of an ability that normally rolls 2d10, you would instead roll 2d8, as d8 is one size down from d10. Conversely, when something notes to _increase_ the dice size of an ability that normally rolls 3d4, you would instead roll 3d6. Dice cannot be brought down below d4 nor higher than d12 by these types of effects.
## Favor & Disfavor
Typically a character will receive favor when the situation is to their advantage. Conversely, a character will receive disfavor when the situation is dire.

Both favor and disfavor rolls will use your [[3. Character Creation#Prowess|prowess]] to decide the size of the dice you roll for both. Typically all characters will start at [[3. Character Creation#Prowess|prowess]] 1 and get to a higher [[3. Character Creation#Prowess|prowess]] as they level up. Below is the dice size you roll for favor _and_ disfavor, based on your current [[3. Character Creation#Prowess|prowess]]:
- Prowess 1: d4
- Prowess 2: d6
- Prowess 3: d8
- Prowess 4: d10
- Prowess 5: d12

Both favor and disfavor may be applied to every roll in the game: [[#Skill checks|Skill checks]], [[#Damage Types|damage rolls]], and so on. You apply favor and disfavor when the rules call for it, or the GM imposes it based on circumstances. Favor and disfavor only apply to the current roll being made. Once the roll is resolved, you no longer get favor or disfavor for further rolls, unless there is a persistent effect in place that continuously imposes one or the other.

>**Important:** Favor and disfavor cancel each other out. Regardless of how many instances of either you have. If you have both favor and disfavor for a single roll, you make the roll as if you had neither. Additionally, neither favor nor disfavor may stack, you can only roll one favor or disfavor die.
## Helping
At any point a character is free and wants to help, they may do so by **expending 1 energy** to roll their [[3. Character Creation#Prowess|prowess die]]. If multiple characters do this, you only keep the single highest roll among the assisting characters.

>**Important:** Before the roll is made, each character must declare their intent to help, then the roll is made to see the result.
# Combat
You will inevitably end up in a situation where you must defend yourself in combat. When this happens, you will need to figure out a plan of action as soon as possible! You certainly don’t want to be caught out alone, surrounded by enemies.
## Encounters
These are divided into rounds. The players’ side and GM’s side alternate, taking their actions. Players go first unless surprised. Players choose who acts when it’s the party’s turn. Once a character has used their actions, they are done for the round. If the enemies outnumber the party, the GM will perform 2 turns, always using the weakest enemy with actions available for their second turn.
### Rounds
A round is started when the encounter starts. Each character gets the opportunity to use their actions within each round before a new round begins. Some status effects may increase or decrease the number of actions a character may perform.

>**Example:** An encounter starts with a pack of wolves. Neither group was surprised, so the PC party goes first. They decide which of them will act, so _Player A_ steps up, performing their action(s). Once their actions conclude, the turn goes to the GM, who controls the wolves. Because the wolves outnumber the party, the GM will take 2 turns, moving and performing action(s) with two separate wolves. Once the actions conclude with the wolves, the turn goes back to the party. Because _Player A_ has already performed all actions that they are able to in a single round, _Player A_ may not do any further actions. Repeat this until all characters in the encounter has performed their actions. Once done, a new round begins and every character gains their actions back and the whole process repeats.
## Surprise Round
In the case that a group (or person) gets the drop on the other, this will start a surprise round! What this means is the surprising party gets to act on “round 0” (the surprise round), and any character that was surprised may not perform any actions this round.

>**Example** **of a NPC surprise round:** A group of kobolds sneak up on the party while they are sleeping and attack! The party is surprised. The kobolds get to perform a round of actions on round 0. The PCs cannot act until round 1 as they were surprised.

>**Example** **of a PC surprise round:** The party sees a group of drunk bandits and sneaks up on them. A PC with a bow performs an attack and the others charge in at them! The bandits are surprised and cannot perform any actions for round 0. The bandits cannot act until round 1 as they were surprised.
## Actions
Each character gets **2 actions per** **round** from the following:
- **Ability.** Perform an ability that your character knows. Many abilities require **2** **actions** to use, but some may require **1 action** instead. 
- **Defend. 2 Actions.** Guard yourself from attackers. You gain [[#Favor & Disfavor|favor]] to block, dodge, and resistance rolls.
- **Item. 2 Actions.** Use an item in your inventory.
- **Sprint. 2 Actions.** Gain additional move speed equal to your current move speed until the end of this turn. 
	- **Example:** A character with 5 move speed can move up to 10 spaces instead when they take this action on their turn.
- **Weapon Attack. 2 Actions.** Perform an attack with your equipped weapon.
- **Wrestle. 2 Actions.** Attempt to grab or push a character. You must make a **MGT** roll and your target must make an **AGI or MGT** roll (pick one). The higher roll wins; reroll ties. If you successfully grab a character, that character becomes grappled.
- **Improvise.** Describe an action that doesn’t specifically fit any of the above actions. The GM determines if the action is possible, whether it’s 1 or 2 actions, the roll, and the DR (if applicable).

Notice that each action has a noted **“x Action(s).”** Each action you perform will either cost 1 action or 2 actions. If you use 1 action during your turn, you may not perform another action that requires 2 actions. Every character abides by this 2 action system.

>**Important:** During combat, any **unused actions** from your turn are lost, so be sure to make full use of your two actions!
## Reactions
Reaction abilities cost some amount of energy to perform. Block and dodge rolls **are** **not** considered reactions. All reactions have a prerequisite that must be true before you may perform it. You may only perform one reaction at a time, even if the situation enables more than one reaction to activate.

Be sure to remember the following statement: **“One reaction per interaction”**

>**Example:** A PC has the two reactions [[Heavy Strike]] and [[Taunting Strike]] and performs a melee weapon attack. Because the prerequisite is fulfilled for both [[Heavy Strike]] and [[Taunting Strike]], you may use either for this attack, but you must choose one or the other as it is part of the same interaction.
## Focus
If you use an ability that denotes “**focus**,” it means your character must maintain focus on that ability. The ability will persist as long as you maintain this focus, up to the ability’s limit. You may stop focusing on any ability that you’ve used whenever you wish as a **free action**. When focusing on an ability, you may still move freely unless explicitly stated otherwise. Each time you take any damage during focus, you must roll an **END** check. **The DR of this ability check is either 8 or the total damage received, whichever is higher (maximum of 18)**. You may only focus on one ability at a time.
## Weapon Mastery
Your ability with a weapon is measured with a “weapon mastery.” From lowest to highest, the ranks are E → D → C → B → A. If your weapon mastery is at E, you are a complete beginner with that specific weapon group; your performance using it is reflected by the ability associated to it. The following chart breaks down the weapon damage scaling. It is universal for all weapons. What separates them is their individual abilities.

| Rank | Damage Roll |
| ---- | ----------- |
| E    | 1d4 (2.5)   |
| D    | 1d6 (3.5)   |
| C    | 1d8 (4.5)   |
| B    | 1d10 (5.5)  |
| A    | 1d12 (6.5)  |
Among these are multiple weapon types that rank up individually from each other: **Blunt, Magic, Profane, Ranged, Sacred, Slash, Stab, and Unarmed.**
## Movement and Spacing
Each tile in The Traveler’s Log is measured using a unit called **yuels**. If a character has a move speed of 3, that means they can move 3 yuels (or 3 tiles) before they either have to use the sprint action or wait for their next turn to move more.

In the case of climbing, unless the character has a specified climbing speed, they can climb a number of yuels equal to half their normal move speed. Some characters may have flight and can simply move their flying speed up a cliff.
## Positional Effects
These are states of being that are circumstantial. Some abilities may interact with these.
- **Dueling.** If you are within 1 yuel of a hostile character and no other characters are within 1 yuel of either of you, both you and your opponent are now dueling. This doesn’t have any immediate effects to either character.
- **Threatened.** If there is a hostile character within 1 yuel of yourself, you are threatened. While you are threatened, all of your ranged attacks and ranged abilities suffer disfavor.
- **Flanking.** If there are two enemies on opposite sides of you and no allies next to you (within 1 yuel of yourself), then you suffer disfavor when attempting to block or dodge attacks.
	- **Example**: In the case that one enemy is south of you and another is east of you, they **would** **not** be flanking you. If an enemy was east of you and another west of you, they **would be** flanking you.
- **Surrounded.** This is a step up from flanking. You cannot be surrounded and flanked at the same time; it’s one or the other. If there are 4 or more hostile characters surrounding you and no allies within 1 yuel of yourself, then you suffer disfavor when attempting to block or dodge attacks and damage rolls against you by the hostiles surrounding you gain favor.
- **Prone.** While you are prone, ranged attacks against you suffer disfavor and melee attacks against you gain favor. To get up costs half of your current move speed. You can always get up if you have at least 1 move speed.
## Unstable Terrain
Sometimes you will encounter rocky cliffs, quicksand, caltrops, and more! This is referred to as “unstable terrain”. When you attempt to move through unstable terrain, you will use 2 yuels of movement per space traveled instead of 1. Some abilities can also cause temporary unstable terrain. In the event that you only have 1 yuel of movement in unstable terrain, you simply cannot move.
## Fall Damage
Fall damage is very simple and follows this pattern: For every 2 yuels a character falls, that character will take 1d6 physical damage upon impact. Fall damage stops scaling at 10d6 (20 yuels). A character falls 10 yuels per round.

>**Note:** Only innate defense against physical damage will reduce physical damage taken this way. Block rolls have no effect against fall damage.
## Defense Rolls & Innate Defense
When a character performs an action that would deal damage to a target. The target has the opportunity to **block** or **dodge** the attack. Once the damage is rolled, the target then chooses how they respond. Blocking is safer but only mitigates the damage (to a minimum of 1) whereas dodging is all or nothing; high risk, high reward.

Block and dodge rolls are the front line of defense. Innate defenses are the back line of defense. Resistance rolls have the unique role of preventing additional effects from happening from abilities. When attacked, you must first go through the front line defensive rolls (block and dodge), then follow up with resistance rolls (if applicable), and finally innate defense.
### Block Rolls
Every PC has a base **block roll** of 1d4. Armor and shields can improve this drastically. Some abilities or attacks can only be blocked, and others cannot be blocked at all. You cannot lower the damage below 1 with your block roll, no matter how high you roll compared to the attack roll (although, innate defense can get around this). When an action is targeting you that cannot be blocked, you may still attempt to block it, however, your roll gets completely ignored and you take the full damage roll instead. In cases where you have a chance to roll below 0 (negative numbers), any value below 0 is still treated as if you rolled a 0.

The higher your block roll is compared to the damage roll, the more remarkable your defense looks like to the attacker. Matching the damage number with your block roll reveals you to have a _sturdy_ defense! When you choose to block, you roll your block dice (1d4+equipped armor+equipped shield). After this is rolled, you then subtract your total roll from the attacker’s roll. Remember, blocked damage cannot be reduced below 1!

>**Example:** An enemy targets you with an attack that is about to deal 5 damage. You choose to block and rolled a total of 3. The damage gets reduced by 3 and you only take 2 damage for this attack. Your defense is fairly solid against your attacker and they feel it.

>**Another example for high block rolls:** An enemy targets you with an attack that is only going to deal 2 damage. You choose to block and rolled a total of 5. Normally they would do “negative damage” by rules of standard math, but because of the rules to blocking you take 1 damage. Your defense is solid against your attacker and they recognize your defense immediately!
### Dodge Rolls
Every PC has a base **dodge roll** of 1d8. Some abilities or attacks can only be dodged, and others cannot be dodged at all. When an action is targeting you that cannot be dodged, you may still attempt to dodge it, however, your roll gets completely ignored and you take the full damage roll instead. When you choose to dodge, you must roll equal to or higher than the damage rolled against you. If you roll equal to or higher than the damage, you dodge the attack and take 0 damage.

The higher your dodge roll is compared to the damage roll, the more remarkable your evasion looks like to the attacker. Matching the damage number with your dodge roll is a _narrow dodge_, only just avoiding the attack!

>**Example:** An enemy targets you with an attack that is about to deal 4 damage. You feel confident you can dodge this attack, so you choose to dodge and rolled an 8. Because you rolled twice the attack’s damage, your dodge looks flawless to your attacker and you take 0 damage.

>**Example of a failed dodge roll:** An enemy targets you with an attack that is about to deal 7 damage. You decide to take the risk and go all out with a dodge roll. Unfortunately your luck runs out in this moment as you rolled a 6. Because you didn’t roll at least equal to the attack’s damage, you suffer the full damage of the attack, taking 7 damage directly! Your evasion is close to dodging the attack, but the attack manages to hit you!
### Resistance Rolls
When an ability calls for resistance, it will denote what skill is tied to the resistance. When this happens, the defender must roll a skill check using the required stat as the modifier for that roll. If the roll is not equal to or higher than the target number, the defender fails the roll.

>**Example:** An evil wizard casts _Charm_ on you. You must make a **will resistance roll** and either meet or beat 10 to resist being charmed.
### Innate Defense
Finally we have innate defense. These are the most difficult to increase as they are guaranteed damage reduction (in most cases). 1 point of physical innate defense means any physical damage you fail to dodge or block successfully gets reduced here by 1 anyways. Similar to block rolls, innate defense is clamped in that your innate defense cannot reduce damage received below 0.

Innate defense covers every damage type individually. Having **1** **Innate Physical defense** means you will always take 1 less physical damage from attacks, but this reduction doesn’t apply to other damage types such as arcane, booming, electric, etc.

There are 3 groups of defense and within those 3 groups are further broken down.

| Normal   | Elemental | Special |
| -------- | --------- | ------- |
| Physical | Electric  | Dark    |
| Arcane   | Fire      | Holy    |
| Booming  | Freezing  | Mental  |
| ———      | Poison    | Soul    |
If you have 2 **Innate Normal defense**, that means your physical, arcane, and booming damage taken is reduced by 2 each. If you have **3** **Innate Freezing defense**, then your freezing damage taken is reduced by 3, but not electric, fire, and poison.

The 3 groups stack with their individual innate defenses as well. If you have an **Innate Elemental defense of 1** and an **Innate Poison defense of 1**, you will take 2 reduced damage to poison instead of 1.

>**Note:** Unlike block and dodge rolls, a negative innate defense will _increase_ damage you receive instead of zeroing out. If you have an innate physical defense of -1 and take 2 points of physical damage after defensive rolls, it becomes 3 physical damage instead.

>**Block** **Example:** An enemy rolls 10 physical damage and you roll 4 to block. With an **Innate Physical defense of 1**, the damage is reduced down to 5.

>**Dodge example:** An enemy rolls 12 electric damage and you roll 9 to dodge. With an **Innate Electric defense of 2**, you only suffer 10 damage as you failed to dodge, but your innate defense still covers some of the damage received.
### Damage Modifiers
Some characters possess specialized defenses, with unique vulnerabilities or immunities to specific damage types.
- **Fatal**: The character takes five times the normal damage from that type.
- **Vulnerable**: The character takes twice the normal damage from that type.
- **Resistant**: The character takes half the normal damage from that type.
- **Immune**: The character takes no damage from that type.
- **Absorbs**: The character takes no damage and instead heals for half of the rolled damage amount.

These modifiers are always applied absolutely last to damage calculation. The order of damage goes as follows: **Damage Roll → Defensive Roll (Block or Dodge) → Innate Defenses → Modifiers**
### Damage Types
- **Physical.** Swords, daggers, bows, hammers, etc.
- **Arcane.** Raw magical energy.
- **Booming.** Sound or shock waves.
- **Electric.** Electricity or lightning bolts.
- **Fire.** Flames, magma, extreme heat.
- **Freezing.** Ice and extreme cold.
- **Poison.** Coated weapons and poisonous creatures.
- **Dark.** Corruption.
- **Holy.** Divine.
- **Mental.** Damage that directly attacks the mind.
- **Soul.** Damage that directly attacks the soul.
### Damage Properties
- **Unblockable:** When this appears, the damage cannot be affected by a block roll. Innate defense still applies.  
    **Example:** “**1d6 poison damage (unblockable)**” means the 1d6 Poison damage cannot be reduced by a block roll, but may still be avoided with a dodge roll.
- **Unavoidable:** When this appears, the damage cannot be affected by a dodge roll. Innate defense still applies.  
    **Example:** “**1d10 holy damage (unavoidable)**” means the 1d10 Holy damage cannot be avoided with an equal or higher dodge roll, but may still be reduced with a block roll.
- **Surefire:** When this appears, the damage cannot be affected by a block nor dodge roll. Innate defense still applies.  
	**Example:** “**1d4 soul damage (surefire)**” means that the 1d4 Soul damage will always be applied, regardless of if the defender rolls to block or dodge it.
- **X Weapon Attack**: This will call a weapon attack for either melee, ranged, or magic weapon attacks. When this appears, you will roll your weapon prowess with a weapon capable of performed the matching attack type. The range of abilities that use a weapon attack is limited to your weapon (usually, some abilities bend this rule a little!).  
    **Example:** Melee weapons with **reach** have a range of 2 yuels. Ranged weapons commonly have a range of 24 yuels. Magic weapons commonly have a range of 12 yuels.

> **Note:** When a character becomes the target of an ability that does **1d6 poison damage (unblockable)** as well as **1d10 holy damage (unavoidable)**, for example, the poison damage cannot be blocked and will not be considered if the defender chooses to block, but the holy damage will be reduced by the block roll. Conversely, if the defender chooses to dodge, the holy damage will not be considered as part of the target number as it ignores dodge rolls.
# Exhaustion
This status can be gained in a myriad of ways: Lack of sleep, over exertion, energy being drained, and so on. Upon finishing a full rest, reduce Exhaustion by one stage.
- **Stage 1.** Your max energy is reduced by half. 
- **Stage 2.** Your move speed is reduced by half.
- **Stage 3.** Your max health is reduced by half.
- **Stage 4.** All rolls are made at disfavor and you cannot benefit from favor. Effects that make you immune to disfavor do not prevent disfavor this way.
- **Stage 5.** All characters gain favor when making any rolls against you.
- **Stage 6.** Your character dies. You do not get a final stand.

Each stage of exhaustion compounds on each other. You apply the effects of every stage below your current stage of exhaustion.

>**Example:** At stage 3 of exhaustion, you also suffer the effects stages 2 and 1 exhaustion in addition to the effects of stage 3 exhaustion.

# Reputation
Every character has a reputation with each settlement and kingdom. Within each settlement – and by extension, the kingdom associated with it – every individual character has a reputation. Reputation is built through actions and interactions.

Poor reputation is earned through lying, cheating, stealing, or other negative behavior. The worse your reputation becomes, the less willing merchants are to do business with you, and the more expensive goods and services become for those that are.

Good reputation is earned through honesty, generosity, reliability, and honorable conduct. With a good reputation, you can haggle for better prices, and people are generally more trusting of you.

Reputation is tracked separately for each settlement and its governing kingdom. A character may be well-regarded in one city and despised in another.
# Soul Condition
Your character’s soul standing represents the state of your character's soul and their relationship with Vero Deus. There are exactly two possible states: _Severed_ or _Connected_. There is no in‑between. When a character reaches 0 HP and is faced with the choice to survive or take a final stand, they experience visions of the realm their soul is bound for based on this. They may embrace their fate and go out in a blaze of glory, or they may continue to survive, knowing that their inevitable destination will eventually catch up with them.
## Severed Soul
A character with severed soul is not necessarily evil in nature, but they have become severed from Vero Deus through their actions or choices. The most common denominator for characters with corrupted moral is cult-ish behavior. These people have dipped their toes into a pact, whether with their self or some nefarious being, it doesn’t matter how. Their soul has been severed and are bound for the nightmare realm at their life’s end.
## Connected Soul
A connected soul is not strictly reserved for miracle workers or healers. It belongs to those who actively seek Vero Deus, walking the Path of the Turned Heart and running the good race of faith to the very end.

These people are sanctified by Vero Deus. Even in hardship, they are at peace, knowing they are held in the hands of their creator. At death, their soul is bound for a heavenly realm, a place of pure peace and unity. It is a utopia beyond mortal craftsmanship, where every soul and heavenly being dwells together in perfect harmony with their creator for all eternity.
## Interactions With Soul Conditions
Soul condition is primarily a flavorful tool to help you imagine your character's near‑death experience. It gives you a sense of what they might see, feel, or encounter when they wake from surviving a dangerous situation, or when they finally give up their soul and pass into eternity.

Despite this, very specific effects look at this and have a different outcome based on it. Primarily: Nightmare and Reverent characters will often look at this and make different choices or have different effects on characters based on their soul condition.
